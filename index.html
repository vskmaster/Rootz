Myth vs. fact

Toggle each one. What you've heard isn't always what's true.

"Everyone's doing it."

Actually a myth. Most teens overestimate use by 2–3x. The clear majority of young people never try illegal drugs at all — you're in that majority, not the minority.

"One try can't hurt."

Partly a fact, partly risk. A single use rarely causes addiction — but a developing brain (under ~25) responds more strongly to substances, and a bad reaction, overdose, or dangerous mix can happen on a first try. Risk isn't the same as safety.

"Addiction means weak willpower."

Myth. Addiction changes brain chemistry around reward and stress — it's a medical condition, not a character flaw. That's exactly why treatment, not shame, is what works.

"Asking for help means you failed."

Myth. Reaching out early is one of the strongest predictors of long-term recovery. It's not the failure — it's the first root taking hold.
